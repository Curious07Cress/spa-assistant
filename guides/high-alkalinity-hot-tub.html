How to Lower Total Alkalinity in a Hot Tub

Quick answerAdd pH decreaser (sodium bisulfate / dry acid) in small doses with jets on, wait 2–4 hours, retest, repeat. Target 80–120 ppm. Alkalinity drops in steps and pH will sag along the way — finish alkalinity first, then aerate (jets on, cover off) to bring pH back up without re-raising alkalinity.

Why high alkalinity matters

Total alkalinity (TA) is your water's pH buffer. Above ~120 ppm, pH gets locked high, sanitizer efficiency drops, and calcium precipitates — that's the cloudy water and crusty scale on the waterline. High-TA water also drifts upward relentlessly, so you keep adding acid anyway.

Step-by-step

  1. Test TA and pH. Note your tub volume (a typical 6-person spa is ~350–450 gallons).
  2. With jets on, add pH decreaser per label for a ~10–20 ppm TA drop. For a 400-gallon tub that's typically in the range of 1–2 oz — always follow your product's label.
  3. Wait 2–4 hours with the pump circulating, then retest.
  4. Repeat until TA reads 80–120 ppm. Several small doses beat one big one — a big acid dose can crash pH into corrosive territory.
  5. Now fix pH: run jets with the cover off in 15–30 minute bursts. Aeration off-gasses CO₂ and raises pH without raising TA. This is the trick most owners miss.
Add chemicals to water, never water to chemicals, and never mix chemicals together dry. Keep the cover open for a while after acid additions.

Common questions

What should hot tub alkalinity be?

80–120 ppm total alkalinity, with pH between 7.2 and 7.8.

Will lowering alkalinity also lower pH?

Yes, temporarily. Finish lowering TA first, then raise pH by aerating with the jets — aeration raises pH without raising TA.

Why does my alkalinity keep going up?

Usually high-alkalinity fill water. Test your tap water; if it's 200+ ppm TA, expect to lower TA after every top-up, or pre-treat fill water.
