Why Your Hot Tub Smells Like Chlorine (It's Not Too Much Chlorine)

Quick answerThat sharp "chlorine" smell is chloramines: chlorine that has already combined with sweat, oils, and ammonia and is now useless as sanitizer. The fix is counterintuitive — shock the tub to oxidize the chloramines. Properly sanitized water has almost no smell.

What you're actually smelling

Free chlorine (the good kind) is nearly odorless at spa levels. When chlorine reacts with nitrogen compounds from sweat, urine, and cosmetics, it forms chloramines — weak sanitizers with a harsh, eye-stinging smell. Strong smell = high combined chlorine = your sanitizer is spent. Hot water and a closed cover concentrate the odor further.

Clear it out

  1. Test. If your kit distinguishes free vs. total chlorine, a big gap between them confirms chloramines.
  2. Balance pH to 7.2–7.4 so the shock works efficiently.
  3. Shock with dichlor or MPS at label dose (breakpoint oxidation destroys chloramines). Bromine tubs get the same treatment — shock regenerates the bromide bank.
  4. Leave the cover off for 30–60 minutes with jets on. The oxidized compounds need to gas off; trapping them under the cover defeats the shock and degrades the cover's underside.
  5. Retest after a few hours; free chlorine 1–3 ppm (bromine 3–5 ppm) with no harsh smell means you're done.

Keep it from coming back

  • Shock after heavy-use sessions, not just on a calendar.
  • Shower before soaking — most chloramine precursors ride in on skin.
  • Air the tub out weekly: cover off, jets on, 20 minutes.
  • A persistent musty smell despite good numbers usually means biofilm in the plumbing — run a line purge before your next drain.
If the smell is musty or sour rather than sharp, that's biofilm or bacteria, not chloramines — do a line flush, drain, and refill.

Common questions

Does a strong chlorine smell mean too much chlorine?

No — usually the opposite. The smell is chloramines, which form when chlorine is used up. The fix is shocking the water, not adding less sanitizer.

Why do my eyes sting in the hot tub?

Chloramines and off-range pH are the two usual causes. Shock the water and keep pH between 7.2 and 7.8.

How often should I shock my hot tub?

Weekly for average use, plus after any heavy-use session with multiple bathers.
