White Flakes and Scale in Your Hot Tub

Quick answerCrusty deposits on the waterline and heater are calcium scale from hard water and high pH — fix with a stain-and-scale remover plus balancing. Soft white flakes that crush to slime between your fingers are biofilm from the plumbing — fix with a line purge, drain, and refill. The finger test tells you which you have.

The finger test

Fish out a flake and rub it. Gritty and hard = calcium carbonate scale. It comes from high calcium hardness (over ~250 ppm), high pH, and high alkalinity pushing minerals out of solution — heat makes it worse, which is why the heater scales first. Soft, slimy, smears like wet tissue = biofilm, colonies of bacteria shed from inside the plumbing, common after sanitizer lapses or in tubs that sat unused.

Fix calcium scale

  1. Test calcium hardness, pH, and TA.
  2. Add a stain & scale remover per label; it dissolves deposits and holds minerals in solution.
  3. Bring pH down to 7.2–7.4 and TA to 80–100 — scale can't form in slightly acidic, low-buffer water.
  4. Clean the filter; it's catching scale dust.
  5. If your fill water is very hard, pre-filter refills or blend with softened water. Weekly scale remover doses keep hard-water tubs clear.

Fix biofilm flakes

  1. Add a line purge product and run jets per label — expect ugly gunk to surface. That's the point.
  2. Drain completely, wipe the shell, rinse.
  3. Clean or replace the filter (biofilm colonizes it).
  4. Refill, balance, and hold sanitizer at the top of range for the first week.
Flakes shortly after switching sanitizer systems or from a mineral cartridge are common — purge and refill rather than dosing chemicals at it for weeks.

Common questions

What are the white flakes floating in my hot tub?

Hard, gritty flakes are calcium scale from hard or high-pH water. Soft flakes that smear are biofilm from the plumbing. Rub one between your fingers to tell.

Will vinegar remove hot tub scale?

Vinegar works on removable parts like filter housings, but for the tub itself use a spa-rated stain and scale product — it treats the whole water body and won't tank your chemistry unpredictably.

What causes biofilm in a hot tub?

Sanitizer lapses, long idle periods, and plumbing that has never been purged. Biofilm lives inside the pipes where sanitizer barely reaches, so a purge product is required to strip it.
